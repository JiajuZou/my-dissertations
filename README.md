## 1. Undergraduate Thesis

### Title

Effect of Duct-End Reflection Coefficient on Axial-Fan Noise

### Abstract

As the aviation industry has grown rapidly, aircraft noise has become an increasingly prominent concern. Fan noise from the engine, a core aircraft component, is a major source of aircraft noise. When designing or predicting the acoustic performance of duct-like systems such as engine inlets, the magnitude of the reflection coefficient at the duct opening provides an important measure of the system's sound radiation capability. Reflection also affects acoustic measurements at the duct wall. Investigating its effects on the in-duct sound field and far-field sound radiation can therefore inform engineering applications.

This thesis uses the finite element solver in the commercial software package COMSOL to simulate ducts with different opening geometries. First, flanged ducts are modeled using the Pressure Acoustics interface. Reflection coefficients for different flange radii are obtained by acoustic impedance integration and compared with published results. The close agreement supports the feasibility of the simulation method. A bellmouth duct is then modeled using the Linearized Potential Flow interface, and the corresponding reflection coefficients are extracted by modal decomposition. Error perturbations are introduced into the simulated pressure data, and the resulting estimates are compared with existing experimental results to assess possible sources of discrepancy. The incident sound field obtained by modal decomposition is also reconstructed for verification. Finally, the influence of the reflection coefficient on standing waves inside the duct is investigated experimentally.

### Keywords

Duct acoustics; Finite element method; Acoustic reflection coefficient; Modal decomposition
